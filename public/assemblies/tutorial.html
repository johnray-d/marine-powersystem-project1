SECTION 04

Generator Paralleling & Synchroscope Lab

Before connecting an incoming diesel generator to a live 6.6 kV bus, an ETO must match frequency, voltage, and phase angle (`Δθ ≈ 0°`, 12 o'clock on the synchroscope) to prevent destructive out-of-phase torque:

  1. Click Scenario 05 · Parallel & sync DG 2 (or click the SYNC button on any offline generator while the 6.6 kV bus is live).
  2. Watch the rotating Synchroscope needle. If the needle rotates clockwise (FAST), incoming frequency is slightly higher than the bus.
  3. Use Gov −0.05 Hz / Gov +0.05 Hz to adjust the incoming generator's speed, or click Auto-Sync to engage automatic governor speed matching.
  4. When the needle enters the green 12 o'clock window (|Δθ| < 15°, |Δf| < 0.35 Hz), click Close Breaker (ACB) to parallel the generator onto the 6.6 kV bus!

SECTION 05

PMS Protection, Blackout Simulation, Earth Faults & Alarm Logbook

Test marine Power Management System (PMS) protection relays and casualty recovery procedures using the PMS Protection & Faults panel:

  • Auto-Standby DG Start: When enabled, if a blackout occurs or bus load exceeds 85%, the PMS automatically cranks, synchronizes, and closes the next standby generator after a 3.5 s start delay.
  • Pref. Trip (>94%): Automatically sheds non-essential hotel loads (Accommodation HVAC, Galley, Reefer sub-bus, and 220 V hotel outlets) before an overload trips the main generators.
  • Trip Online DG: Simulates an immediate mechanical/electrical trip of an online main generator to test load transfer or blackout recovery.
  • 6.6 kV & 440 V Earth Fault Injection: Simulates insulation breakdown (`IR = 0.02 MΩ` on 440 V or `IR = 0.08 MΩ` on 6.6 kV) and triggers an Insulation Monitoring Device (IMD) alarm in the ETO Alarm Logbook.

SECTION 06

Symmetrical Dual-Transformer Architecture & 3-Tier Split-Bus Ties

The electrical architecture has been fully upgraded to a redundant Symmetrical Dual-Transformer Topology reflecting modern marine class rules (DNV-GL / Lloyd's Register DP-2 classification):

  • Dual 6.6 kV / 440 V Main Step-Down Transformers (2.5 MVA each): TX A (Port) feeds 440 V Bus A; TX B (Starboard) feeds 440 V Bus B. If one transformer trips, the 440 V Bus Tie automatically allows the surviving transformer to carry all critical hotel and auxiliary loads.
  • Dual 6.6 kV / 440 V Dedicated Reefer Transformers (1.5 MVA each): TX REEFER A & B feed refrigerated container boards directly from 6.6 kV, isolating heavy compressor motor inrushes from sensitive engine room auxiliaries.
  • Dual 440 V / 220 V Control & Hotel Transformers (500 kVA each): TX CON A & B provide galvanized isolation and voltage step-down for Navigation Bridge electronics, Galley, and Accommodation lighting.
  • Three Symmetrical Bus Ties:
    • 6.6 kV Bus Tie: Operates between Bus A and Bus B for synchronized parallel generation or split-bus islanding.
    • 440 V Bus Tie: Links Port 440 V Bus A and Starboard 440 V Bus B.
    • 220 V Bus Tie: Links 220 V Sub-Board A and Sub-Board B for complete domestic lighting redundancy.

SECTION 07

ANSI Protective Relay Engine (ANSI 32, ANSI 40 & ANSI 51 IDMT Curves)

The simulator implements high-fidelity IEEE / ANSI protective relay algorithms governing all 6.6 kV diesel generators and heavy feeder breakers:

  • ANSI 32 (Reverse Power Protection): If a generator's prime mover suffers fuel starvation or mechanical drag while paralleled, the generator motors and draws active power from the live busbar. When active power draws \(P < -0.05 \times P_{\text{rated}}\) (\(-5\%\)) for \(> 3.0\text{ seconds}\), the relay instantly trips the generator vacuum circuit breaker (VCB) with an "ANSI 32: Reverse Power Trip" alarm.
  • ANSI 40 (Loss of Excitation / Field Failure): If an Automatic Voltage Regulator (AVR) fails or rotor field excitation collapses, the synchronous machine absorbs large amounts of inductive reactive power (\(Q < -0.20 \times Q_{\text{rated}}\)) accompanied by terminal voltage sag below \(85\%\) nominal (\(5.61\text{ kV}\)). The relay trips the breaker after a \(1.5\text{ s}\) timer with "ANSI 40: Loss of Field". You can simulate this using the AVR / Field Trip toggle in the generator card.
  • ANSI 51 (IDMT Inverse-Time Overcurrent Curve): Instantaneous hard trips are replaced with the IEC 60255 Standard Inverse IDMT equation:
    t = k / [ (I / I_s)^α − 1 ]     (where k = 0.14, α = 0.02, I_s = 1.10 × I_rated)
    Transient motor starting inrushes safely ride through without nuisance tripping, while sustained overcurrents trip the feeder inversely proportional to the fault magnitude.
Fig 7 — Protective Relay Engine showing live ANSI 32 Reverse Power, ANSI 40 Loss of Field status with AVR Trip simulator, and IEC Standard Inverse (ANSI 51) IDMT time-overcurrent curve readout.
SECTION 08

High-Resistance Earthing (HRE) Architecture & ANSI 67N Directional Ground Fault

Marine high-voltage installations require specialized earthing architectures to control ground fault currents while maintaining service continuity:

  • System Architecture Toggle: ETOs can toggle between "Unearthed IT System" (classic commercial marine setup with high-capacitance ground paths) and "High-Resistance Earthed (HRE) System" (modern SOLAS offshore DP standard).
  • Neutral Earthing Resistor (NER = 381 Ω): In HRE mode, the 6.6 kV transformer and generator neutrals are grounded to the vessel's hull structure through a calibrated \(381\ \Omega\) stainless steel resistor bank.
  • Fault Current Limited to 10.0 A: When a phase ground fault occurs, the fault current is clamped strictly to:
    I_fault = V_(L-N) / R_ner = 3,810 V / 381 Ω = 10.0 A
    This prevents catastrophic iron core destruction and restriking overvoltages while allowing machinery to run safely until an orderly shutdown.
  • Neutral Point Displacement & ANSI 67N: Neutral voltage shifts to full line-to-neutral potential (\(V_N = 3,810\text{ V}\)), triggering the ANSI 67N Directional Earth Fault Alarm in the PMS telemetry drawer.
Fig 8 — High-Resistance Earthing (HRE) drill showing 381 Ω NER active, fault current clamped to 10.0 A, neutral displacement V_N = 3,810 V, and ANSI 67N Directional Earth Fault alarm.
SECTION 09

SOLAS Blackout Recovery 5-Stage Sequential Waterfall & HUD Progress Bar

Under SOLAS Chapter II-1 regulations, reconnecting all electrical consumers simultaneously upon restoring switchboard busbars causes destructive inrush current spikes and generator blackout re-trips. The simulator enforces a strict 5-stage sequential restoration waterfall:

  1. Stage 1 (T = 0s – 10s) — Emergency DG to ESB: Emergency Diesel Generator cranks and connects within 10 seconds to restore Emergency Switchboard (ESB) loads: Steering Gear, Navigation Radios, Radar, and Escape Route Lighting.
  2. Stage 2 (T = 10s – 20s) — Main DG & Stage 1 Auxiliaries: Main DG 1 synchronizes onto the 6.6 kV bus, restoring Main Engine Lube Oil and Jacket Water Circulating pumps.
  3. Stage 3 (T = 20s – 30s) — Stage 2 Auxiliaries: Sea Water Cooling and Heavy Fuel Oil (HFO) Booster supply pumps energize once lube oil pressure stabilizes.
  4. Stage 4 (T = 30s – 45s) — Essential Services: Starting Air Compressors and Auxiliary Boiler feed pumps restart to prepare for full propulsion readiness.
  5. Stage 5 (T > 45s) — Non-Essential & Heavy Loads: Engine Room Ventilation Fans, Air Conditioning Chillers, Deck Cargo Cranes, and Aft / Bow Propulsion Thrusters are released for operation.

A dynamic visual progress bar at the bottom HUD displays the active stage name, countdown timer, percentage completion, and load shedding indicators during restoration.

Fig 9 — SOLAS Blackout Recovery 5-Stage Sequential Waterfall active with dynamic HUD progress bar showing stage progression from emergency steering gear up to heavy propulsion thrusters.
SECTION 10

Interactive 6.6 kV Permit to Work (PTW) & 5-Step LOTO Isolation Sequence

In compliance with high-voltage marine safety codes (IEC 60092-503 and High Voltage Permit to Work frameworks), opening cubicle doors or exploding 3D CAD assemblies of 6.6 kV transformers, switchboards, or propulsion motors strictly requires completion of a 5-step safety isolation protocol:

  1. Step 1 — Trip & Open Feeder VCB: Disconnect the circuit breaker feeding the high-voltage component.
  2. Step 2 — Rack Out VCB Truck to 'Test / Isolated': Withdraw the draw-out vacuum breaker truck to physically separate primary disconnect stabs behind grounded safety shutters.
  3. Step 3 — Apply Padlock & LOTO Danger Tag: Lock the racking mechanism shutter with a physical padlock and authorized safety lockout tag.
  4. Step 4 — Prove Dead with 6.6 kV Capacitive Wand: Test the conductor terminals with a rated 6.6 kV capacitive voltage detector wand (verified against an approved proving unit immediately prior).
  5. Step 5 — Close Circuit Main Earth (CME) Switch: Engage the mechanical earthing switch to bond all three high-voltage phases directly to the steel vessel hull.

If an ETO attempts to open or explode an un-isolated 6.6 kV assembly without completing these 5 steps, the simulator displays an active "⚡ ARC-FLASH VIOLATION WARNING" badge on the equipment card.
